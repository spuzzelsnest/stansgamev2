# Unity additions

Drop-in scripts for the Unity project (first-person view, hit feedback, health bar, settings, climbing over low obstacles, and AI that no longer gets stuck behind the gliders). They sit outside `Assets/` on purpose, so a project that pulls this repository does not start compiling them before someone has looked at them. They do not change any existing script, scene or prefab. Most of them use only the game's public API; `Mantle` and `AiUnstick` also read and set a few private fields of `SoldierController` and `SoldierAI` by name (`climbing`, `climbFrom`, `climbTo`, `climbT`, `vel`, `target`, `StartClimb`), so renaming those in the game scripts disables the climbing and unstick features quietly. Once the scripts are in `Assets/`, the cleaner way is to make those members `internal` or add small public methods for them.

## StansGame.Additions

| Script | What it does |
|---|---|
| `AdditionsBootstrap.cs` | Starts itself when the game loads (`RuntimeInitializeOnLoadMethod`) and adds the components below to the local player (`MissionDirector.Instance.player`), also after a respawn: PlayerView, WeaponFeel, HitFeedback, HealthHud, Mantle, RoleKit and SquadCalls. The menu, settings, atmosphere, ragdoll wrists and the bootcamp live on its own object. |
| `PlayerView.cs` | First-person view: camera at the head bone (eyes), head hidden for the local player only, body turns with the view, near clip 0.05. **V** switches first / third person. Applies the field of view (hip and aimed) and mouse sensitivity settings in both views. Aiming (right mouse) in first person brings the weapon up to the eye (`IronSights.cs`, `WeaponPose.cs`): over 0.18 s (pistol) to 0.40 s (Bren), eased, with a small dip and cant on the way up. The camera stays at the eye, the rear sight and the front sight land on the screen centre, and the hands follow through the game's hand IK. The hip crosshair is hidden, and the mouse sensitivity is scaled to the zoom (plus an "Aim sensitivity" setting). `WeaponPose` also straightens the Sten model, which sits 12.9 degrees off its own bore. The pistol is aimed at arm's length (rear sight 0.50 m from the eye), both hands on the grip. The PIAT gets a raised view with a small centre dot. At the hip in first person every gun is held in a fixed pose per type (pistol, SMG, rifle, heavy) in front of the camera, so the M1911 is visible (the game held it against the chest, behind the gloves; `WeaponPose` also raises it 3 cm in the grip). The pistol is held out low in both hands (low ready), so the hands wrap it as when aiming. A switched-to weapon is raised into view over 0.3 s, and while sprinting the weapon drops across the chest. Reloading is shown: the weapon comes down from the eye; a rifle, Sten or Bren is pushed out to the middle of the view, muzzle up and to the left, canted so the action and magazine well face the eyes (a pistol's muzzle tips up beside the face); it jerks down as the empty magazine comes out and up as the full one goes in, and comes back over the game's own reload time. |
| `IronSights.cs` | The sight line for aiming in first person. Weapons whose models carry their own sights are aimed through those, with nothing added: the Sten (rear peep plate 490 mm behind the muzzle, front blade 184 mm) and the M1911 (rear notch and front blade). The points were measured from renders of the bare models, as their meshes cannot be read at run time. A small turn lays the sight line along the view. Other weapons get generated sights (a front post with ears and a rear peep ring, or a blade and notch for a pistol), built once in the weapon's own material, without colliders. The Lee-Enfield (Bren No. 2) uses these: its model's rear aperture is only painted on a closed leaf and sits left of the bore, so it cannot be looked through. |
| `WeaponFeel.cs` | Weapon feel for the local player. Recoil: every shot kicks the view and the weapon (pitch, some yaw and roll, a push into the shoulder, muzzle flip) and both recover in a fraction of a second, per weapon type (the bolt rifle and pistol kick hard, the Sten and Bren in many small kicks; less when aiming, kneeling or prone). The game's own aim climb per shot is kept; this is the visible kick on top. While aiming the kick turns view and weapon together around the eye, so the sights stay lined up. Sway: the weapon trails fast mouse movement and breathes when still. Bob: weapon and eye move with the steps, little while aiming. Hit markers: a tick on every hit and a cross on screen, red and larger with a second knock on a kill, from `Weapon.HitConfirmed`, only for the local player's hits on the enemy; the cross is drawn only while the game's crosshair is hidden (aiming), as the crosshair draws its own at the hip. Bullet cracks: an enemy round (`Weapon.ShotPassed`) passing within 4 m of the head cracks from the side it passed on, shakes the view slightly and darkens the screen edges briefly (suppression). Sounds are generated, no assets. "Weapon motion" in the settings scales kick, sway and bob (0 to 100 %); the shake and darkening follow "Hit effects". |
| `HitFeedback.cs` | When the local player loses health: a hit sound (generated, no asset), a red flash at the screen edges, a short camera flinch, and a marker pointing to where the shot came from. Below 35 % health: heartbeat (faster as health drops) and a pulsing red edge. Detects damage from `Health.Current`, so it also works for co-op clients. |
| `HealthHud.cs` | Health panel bottom left: colour by health, a light segment for the damage just taken, numbers, stance or "reloading", and a key hint line with the player's own keyboard labels (ZQSD on AZERTY). It covers the game's own two text lines there (`MissionDirector.OnGUI`: weapon line at 20, H-60 and health line at 20, H-34 with a fixed "Z prone"), so health and ammo each show once; ammo stays in the game's weapon bar. |
| `SettingsPanel.cs` | **F10** (or Settings in the main menu): view, field of view, aim field of view, mouse sensitivity, hit effects, weapon motion, volume, heartbeat, enhanced night lighting, smooth edges. Pauses the local player's controller while open. Saved in `PlayerPrefs` (`stans.*`). |
| `Mantle.cs` | Climbing over low obstacles (0.45 to 1.7 m: wreckage, crates, wire, low walls) for the local player: press **Space** against one, or keep walking into it for a moment, in any direction (relative to the camera, like the game's own movement). Uses the game's own climb movement (the canal bank one). Never climbs onto a glider. Also holds the shared `Obstacle` test. |
| `AiUnstick.cs` | Gets AI soldiers moving again when they walk into something (the AI has no pathfinding, so the glider sections stayed stuck behind the wreckage). After about a second without progress: climb a low obstacle; or, when advancing or following, plan a route out with `LocalPath` and lead the soldier along it; or sidestep when in a fight. After 8 s still in the same place: put the soldier on the nearest free ground towards where it is going. Only runs where the AI is simulated (offline or co-op host). |
| `FriendlyPassThrough.cs` | The local player and British soldiers no longer block each other's movement (`Physics.IgnoreCollision` between their CharacterControllers; shots are raycasts and still hit as before). A glider section of five could box the player in against the wreckage. |
| `GliderExit.cs` | The player and the glider sections start on the open side of each glider, facing the objective. When a glider has landed and settled, it searches clear, level ground around the real wreck (not on wreckage, a step from the hull, room for the section, open view towards the objective) and moves that glider's `gliderSectionSpawns` point there; soldiers the landing just spawned are moved with it. When the player gets control after the Intro: a short exit (black, the door, a jump down from the glider door onto that ground with a landing dip). Also moves a player who respawns during "Over the bridge" back to the east bank, so dying no longer completes that phase (level 1 review, finding 2). |
| `ObjectiveHud.cs` | Makes the objective clear in game: a marker on the target with what to do and the distance (ATTACK the nearest east-bank defender, TAKE or DEFEND the bridge objective, DESTROY the lead panzer), clamped to the screen edge with a pointer when off screen; a "New objective" banner with a short bugle sting at every phase change; a progress line under the game's objective text (defenders left, signals sent with the H key label, time to hold). |
| `LocalPath.cs` | Small grid pathfinder (0.7 m cells, 44 m square around the soldier) against the real colliders, at most one plan per frame. |
| `MainMenu.cs` | Title screen ("press any key", first launch only), main menu (Play, Level select, Co-op, Settings, Credits, Quit), level select with a briefing built from the level's own mission phases, and credits, over the level while the game is in its start menu. Hides the game's own connection menu (`NetSession`) and brings it back for Co-op. Play and Level select call `NetSession.PlayAlone`, so the Intro film plays as before. Mouse, keyboard (arrows or the move keys, Enter, Esc) and gamepad (d-pad, A, B). After Esc > Leave the game it opens on the main menu. Before a level starts, a role page shows the three roles (`Roles.cs`) as cards with their kit; Left/Right picks, Enter starts, Esc goes back. The bootcamp skips the role page (it hands out its own training kit). The first Play offers the bootcamp once (`Bootcamp.Offer`); the level select always has it, as card "BC" with its nine stations instead of objectives; "on to Pegasus Bridge" on the bootcamp's end card comes back here and opens level 1's role page. |
| `Roles.cs` | The role chosen before the level, with 1944 names for D Company, 2nd Ox and Bucks (glider-borne): **Section Commander** (assault: Sten with 10 magazines, 4 Mills bombs; his calls for help come back sooner), **Stretcher-Bearer** (medic: Sten with 5 magazines, 1 Mills bomb, 8 field dressings put on in 2 s for 100 health, faster recovery; **G** dresses the wounds of a British soldier within 3 m) and **Bren No. 2** (ammunition: Lee-Enfield with 100 rounds, 2 Mills bombs, 4 bandoliers; **G** opens a bandolier for 3 magazines of the weapon in hand). `RoleKit` hands out the kit once the player has control, and again after a respawn, through the game's own `Loadout` and `Weapon.SetAmmo`; the rifle comes from the rifleman prefab through the game's own weapon pickup (`Pickup.DropWeapon` and `Loadout.Take`), and the Sten it replaces is removed. The choice is saved in `PlayerPrefs` (`StansGame.Role`). |
| `SquadCalls.cs` | Calling the section for help, for every role: **T** shouts for a stretcher-bearer (+80 health), **B** for ammunition (2 magazines for the main weapon, 1 for the pistol). The nearest British soldier with a clear run to the player (else the nearest) breaks off, runs over without stopping to fight, hands it over within 3 m and goes back to his own orders. If he makes no headway for 5 s, the next man is sent (up to 3); he can be shot on the way. Each call has its own wait (75 s, 40 s for the Section Commander). A marker shows who is coming and how far off, or which side he comes from. Also draws two small lines top right, under the game's "Playing alone" line: the role with its G action, and both calls with their wait. Only offline or as co-op host (`Net.Simulates`). |
| `MuzzleSmoke.cs` | Smoke from the muzzle on every shot of the local player: a puff that rolls forward, grows and drifts up (bigger for rifles and the Bren), and a thin wisp from the barrel for a second after the last shot. One world-space particle system per weapon, a generated smoke texture, and the `Sprites/Default` shader (the URP particle shaders are stripped from the build). |
| `Atmosphere.cs` | Two settings (F10), both on by default and put back exactly when turned off. **Enhanced night lighting**: three-colour ambient light (moonlit sky, dark ground) instead of one flat colour, a slightly stronger moon with shadows to 70 m, and a global volume (priority 5, under the game's own "Film" volume) with neutral tonemapping, soft bloom on flashes and fires, and a gentle cool grade. **Smooth edges**: 4x MSAA and alpha to coverage on alpha-clipped leaf and grass materials, with their gloss capped, so tree crowns and hedges stop breaking up into flickering pixels. With the source these belong in the lighting settings, the URP asset and the materials. |
| `RagdollWrists.cs` | Relaxes the wrists of the dead. The ragdolls have rigidbodies and joints down to the forearms but none on the hands, so a hand kept the rotation of its last animated frame (with the weapon hand IK, a wrist bent 90 to 150 degrees lay frozen on the ground). When the animator stops, each hand is eased back to its bind-pose rotation over 0.25 s. With the source the better fix is a rigidbody and a CharacterJoint (about 60 degree limits) on each hand. |
| `Bootcamp.cs` | An optional bootcamp of about 5 minutes before level 1, at Countess Wear near Exeter, where D Company rehearsed the bridges in spring 1944. Offered once before the first Play, always in the level select, never required. It runs in level 1's own scene with the night mission held (no Intro film, AI and tanks switched off, night ambience off, daylight and fog set every frame) on a training ground built 500 m off the map. Nine stations, each with a sergeant-major's line, a checklist of steps with the player's own key labels (ZQSD on AZERTY) and a marker: **Fall in** (look left and right, V twice, walk to the flag), **Assault course** (sprint, jump the ditch, climb the wall), **Under the wire** (kneel, prone, crawl under the wire while a Bren fires live rounds overhead with tracer and near-miss cracks and thunderflashes go off beside the lane, kneel behind the sandbags), **The canal** (swim across and climb out), **The range** (pop-up targets, one at a time with a clack: 3 hits with the Sten from the hip at 25 m, take the Lee-Enfield off the table, 3 aimed hits at 50 m, reload, switch to the pistol and back; a missed target drops after 4.5 or 6 s), **Grenades** (a Mills bomb into the pit), **PIAT** (knock out a StuG hulk), **Field dressing** (take a wound, press 4) and **The bridge** (a timed run: over the start line, drop the five pop-up defenders, take the deck and hold H to send Ham and Jam; the PIAT can be swapped for a Sten off the table). **Backspace** skips a station; a death respawns at the station's checkpoint. The end card shows the bootcamp time, the bridge run and the best run (`PlayerPrefs` `stans.bootcampBestRun`); **R** runs the bridge again without recording the bootcamp a second time, **Enter** goes on to Pegasus Bridge. Completing it is saved in `save.json` like a level. The other additions (glider exit, objective HUD, mission watch, role kit, squad calls) stand down while it runs. Every instructor line is raised as the static event `Bootcamp.InstructorLine(id, text)` for a voice to speak: `s0.intro` to `s8.intro`, `s0.praise` to `s7.praise`, `s0.hint` to `s8.hint` (after 30 s without a step done, and again every 30 s), `offer`, `skip` and `end`. |
| `TrainingGround.cs` | Builds the training ground at run time from level 1's own assets plus generated meshes: mown turf, gravel paths, hedges of the level's box shrubs with poplars outside, a grassy range butt, bell tents and an aid tent, a Union flag that flutters, knife-rest wire and the canal revetment from the level, the level's StuG III as the PIAT hulk, a water pit with the canal's water material, registered with the game's own swimming, wooden targets on mounds, a jogging track, start line tape and a footbridge. Materials use the level's textures on metre-based UVs; a sky dome with clouds and a post-processing volume (priority 50) give the daylight look. |
| `TrainingKit.cs` | The building blocks for the ground: lookups for the level's textures, materials, meshes and prefabs; copies of level objects with their scripts, rigidbodies, lights and sounds stripped; generated meshes (metric boxes, prisms, bell tents, grass clumps) and textures (Union flag, red cross, target faces); the sky, ambience (wind, birds, far-off explosions) and a tracer streak drawn with `Sprites/Default` (the game's own tracer shader is stripped from the build, see Known issues). Also `Drill`, which poses and walks the soldier copies: stand, aim, crouch, prone, routes, facing, with the model lowered to the ground for the prone and crouch clips, which hold the body at standing height. |
| `TrainingCrowd.cs` | 41 British soldiers on the sidelines, copies of the game's own prefabs with their AI and network parts removed: recruits fallen in two ranks, others waiting at each station, a Bren gunner firing over the wire, riflemen prone on the range firing at their own targets, a casualty and a medic at the aid post, onlookers at the canal and the bridge, and six men jogging laps of the track. A red-beret sergeant-major walks to each station ahead of the player. |
| `LevelCatalogue.cs` | The levels in the level select (the bootcamp, level 1 Pegasus Bridge; level 2 shown as in development). Level N+1 unlocks when level N is completed; the bootcamp is always open, plays no part in unlocking and is never the "continue" level. Belongs in a ScriptableObject once the source is in. |
| `Progress.cs` | Saved progress in `save.json` next to `Player.log`: completed levels, best time, first completion date. Written to a temporary file and swapped in; a damaged file is kept as `save.json.bad` and the game starts fresh. |
| `MissionWatch.cs` | Marks the level completed when the mission reaches its last phase and the closing film has ended or was skipped, and shows a "Mission complete" card with the time and best time. |
| `GameSettings.cs` | The saved settings. |
| `KeyLabel.cs` | Key names in the active keyboard layout (`Keyboard.current[key].displayName`). |

### Install

1. Copy the `StansGame.Additions` folder into `Assets/` (for example `Assets/Scripts/StansGame.Additions/`).
2. Let Unity compile. Nothing else to set up: no prefab or scene changes.
3. Optional clean-ups in the existing scripts, now that the new HUD shows the same information:
   - remove the `"Health {0}    <size=13>{1}  (C kneel, Z prone)</size>"` line from `MissionDirector.OnGUI` (it shows the wrong key on AZERTY);
   - remove the hard-coded 60 / 45 field of view lerp at the end of `SoldierController.LateUpdate` (`PlayerView` overrides it every frame anyway).

To remove the additions again, delete the folder.

### Tested

There was no Unity source or editor on the testing machine, so the scripts were tested against the demo 0.1 build (Unity 6000.6.8f1):

- compiled without warnings against the build's own `Assembly-CSharp.dll` and Unity modules;
- added to a copy of the build and run in the real game with a test probe (not in this repository) that started "Play alone", waited for the landing, and checked: components attached to the local player; first-person camera 0.13 m from the head bone with the body facing the view; third person back at 2.7 m with the head shown; a 30 % hit gave the flash, the sound and a direction marker on the correct side; at 15 % health the heartbeat played; the F10 panel paused and released the controller. No exceptions in the log.

Second round (scripted play run, intro not skipped, keys and mouse simulated through the Input System on an AZERTY layout): the key labelled Z walked 6.7 m forward, strafe, mouse look, V, C, fire, reload and F10 all worked.

Glider exit: without these scripts, 4 of the 5 British soldiers of glider 1 never moved and the 5th stopped after 6.7 m: they spawn behind the glider and walk straight into invisible 1.1 to 1.4 m high boxes around the wreckage (`Environment/Details from the 1944 photos/Collider`). With `AiUnstick`, all 5 were out and in the fight within 40 s (7 to 50 m from the spawn) in both runs with the final version. The player, walking into the same wreckage, climbed over it and reached the canal bank 21 m on. A play-test build with a movement trace caught the player boxed in between the glider, the wreckage and two British soldiers (a strafe moved 0.1 m); with `FriendlyPassThrough` and climbing in every direction the same strafe moved 3.5 m and the trace logged no more jams.

Without the Unity source, the real fix belongs in the scene as well: a NavMesh for the AI, and section spawn points on the open side of each glider.

Roles, squad calls, smoke, reload and lighting (scripted runs, 13 November 2026, 1280x720, keys called directly as the test window has no focus): each role's kit was checked after the glider exit (Section Commander 9/9, Stretcher-Bearer 10/10, Bren No. 2 14/14 checks). T sent the nearest British soldier, who arrived in 3 to 11 s from 10 to 17 m and dressed the wounds (health 90 to 187 or more); B brought two magazines. In phase 4 the PIAT handover works with the Bren No. 2's kit: as for the Sten, the game swaps the PIAT for the primary and the Lee-Enfield lies at the player's feet to take back. A Lee-Enfield shot left a puff that grows and fades over about 2 s. During a reload the rifle's action and the right hand stay in view (screen shots at 15, 32 and 64 % of the reload). Ragdoll wrists: a German soldier killed in front of the player lay with his hands 64 and 90 degrees off their rest rotation; 3 s later both were at 0. With "Enhanced night lighting" the ambient changed from flat to three-colour and the camera rendered post-processing with 4x MSAA; 6 alpha-clipped leaf and grass materials were switched to alpha to coverage.
Not tested yet: a real play session with mouse and keyboard, co-op with two PCs, and every animation pose in first person (crouch, prone, swimming, climbing out of the canal). In first person the weapon is held where the third-person animation holds it; aiming down the sights would need a sight point per weapon.

### Known issues

- `Tracer.Spawn` (the game's own tracer) throws an `ArgumentNullException` on every tracer shot. It creates its material from `Shader.Find("Universal Render Pipeline/Particles/Unlit")`, and that shader is stripped from the build. The exception shows in the log whenever the player fires; the shot itself still hits. The bootcamp draws its own tracers instead. With the source, the fix is to reference the material in the scene (or add the shader to Always Included Shaders).
